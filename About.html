How I think about your reporting

Five rules that shape every job, and rule some approaches out.

01
Agree the numbers before building dashboards

If "revenue" means three things, a better chart just shows the wrong number faster. Definitions are written down and signed off first.

02
Fix the cause, not the report

A slow month-end is usually an account-mapping or data-entry problem upstream. Fixing that is often cheaper than any new report.

03
Use the simplest tool that works

Sometimes the right answer is one well-built Excel workbook, not a Fabric platform. You get told which, before you spend.

04
Estimated and scoped in writing

You know the hourly rate, the estimated hours and what "done" means before work starts. Changes are agreed, not discovered on the invoice.

05
Built in your account, owned by you

Everything lives in your Microsoft tenant, documented and version-controlled, so you never depend on one person.

What buyers usually underestimate

Where reporting projects go wrong, and what is done about it from day one.

Messy source data

Most of the effort in a reporting project is cleaning and matching data from different systems, not drawing charts. It gets checked in the first week, not discovered at the end.

Who can see what

Report security that works in testing can leak after go-live, especially with guest users and shared workspaces. Each role is tested before launch.

Licence costs

Fabric capacity is not always needed; Power BI Pro is enough for many businesses. The licence plan is part of the quote.

What happens after handover

Reports break when a source system changes. Alerts, documentation and a trained person on your team keep them running.
