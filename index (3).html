Questions your team can finally answer

Once your systems feed one model, questions that used to take a week of spreadsheet work are answered by opening a report. A few we build for most often.

Finance

  • Are we cash positive this month, and how much are we owed?
  • Which customers are overdue, and by how much?
  • How are we tracking against budget, by entity and by cost centre?
  • What does the consolidated P&L look like across all entities, today?

Operations

  • Which site or property is underperforming once you adjust for size?
  • Where does margin actually come from: which products, jobs or services?
  • Which supplier is late, and what did it cost us?
  • What is utilisation this week, by team and by role?

Board and investors

  • What is profit per property, per branch, per entity?
  • What do lenders and investors need to see, and can we produce it on demand?
  • Are we on plan, and where is the variance coming from?

Compliance and outcomes

  • Where does non-compliance concentrate, so inspection effort goes there first?
  • Did the program change outcomes, or just count activity?
  • Can we trace this number on the report back to the source record it came from?

Every number, defined once

The reason two reports disagree is rarely a bug. It's that "revenue" or "active customer" or "on time" means something slightly different in each system. We agree one definition for every measure, write it down, and build it into the model, so finance, operations and the board are reading the same number, and anyone can see how it was calculated.

Questions we get asked first

What does it cost?

We don't bill hourly for delivery and nothing is built without a scope both sides have signed. After the call, a Discovery establishes what's actually required and you receive a written proposal with a fixed price.

How long does it take?

The Discovery takes about two weeks. What follows depends on how many systems are involved and how clean they are; two sources and ten sources are different jobs, so Discovery sets the schedule and the price for the build. Larger projects always start with it. Builds are delivered in phases, so you see something working early regardless of total length.

Do we need Fabric already?

No. Many clients start with Power BI Pro or nothing. Part of Discovery is working out what licensing you actually need, which is often less than you'd be sold. If you bought Fabric capacity and stalled, that's a common and useful starting point.

Do you work with our IT team?

Yes, and it usually works better that way. We hand the modelling and governance layer over as it's built, and if you have a Power BI developer we can set the architecture and standards and let them deliver against it.

What if you're not available later?

Everything is built so you aren't dependent on us: definitions under Git version control, design documented in writing, your team trained to run and extend it. Your reporting doesn't live on a consultant's laptop.

Can you handle confidential or regulated data?

Yes. NDA before discovery, all work inside your tenant, access scoped and removed at handover, row-level security designed in from the start. For government contracts or specific registration requirements, mention them on the booking call and we'll confirm what we can meet before you commit.
