The problem

Why multi-site reporting breaks

Nobody buys a dashboard. They buy the ability to rank their sites and trust the order.

With one site, reporting is arithmetic. With fifteen, it's a definitions problem. Every site keeps its own workbook and defines the measures slightly differently, so the same word means a different number depending on who you ask. Nothing can be compared, because nothing is being measured the same way. I bring the data together and define each measure once, for every site — so the numbers line up and you can see how each one is doing.

Roll-ups that don't reconcile The portfolio total doesn't equal the sum of the sites, and nobody can say which one is wrong.
Unfair comparisons Sites of different sizes ranked on raw totals, so the biggest always looks best and the ranking tells you nothing.
Slow site onboarding A new location takes weeks to appear in reporting because someone has to hand-wire it in.
Fifteen versions of one file Access managed by emailing different workbooks to different managers, with no way to know who has what.

How the work is organised

Engagements usually start in one area and expand into the next. The order is deliberate — governance is worth very little sitting on a pipeline nobody trusts.

01

Every site's data, in one place

Getting every site's data into one governed place automatically, so reporting stops depending on each location remembering to send a file.

The layer that decides whether your sites can be compared at all. Every measure is defined once and applied identically everywhere, so "revenue per unit" means the same thing at site three as it does at site fourteen.

  • Weighted aggregation so comparisons are fair
  • Tie-out reporting against the source system

How I work

Six stages, in this order

You're hiring one person, so you're entitled to know exactly what happens and in what order. Nothing gets built until stages one to three are signed off — most failed reporting projects start building at stage four.

1

Discovery

What decisions is your team actually trying to make, and which numbers are they arguing about? I map the stakeholders, the KPIs that matter, and — the important one — who currently owns each definition. Usually nobody does, which is the root of the problem.

2

Assess

What's really in your systems, as opposed to what everyone believes is in them. Source systems, reporting as it stands today, data quality, and precisely where the numbers start to diverge between sites. You get this in writing whether or not you go further.

3

Design

The architecture, the data model, the measure definitions and who's permitted to see what — agreed and documented before any build starts. This is the stage most projects skip, and it's why their portfolio total never reconciles to the sum of the sites.

4

Build

Delivered in phases with sign-off at each milestone, so you see something working early instead of waiting months for a single hand-over. Ingestion, the modelling layers, the measures and the reporting, built on Microsoft Fabric and Power BI.

5

Validate

The stage that earns the trust. Every figure is tied back to your accounting system line by line, and I show you the reconciliation. If a number doesn't agree, you find out from me first — not from your auditor or your board.

6

Enable

Your team is trained to run and extend it, the model and its definitions are documented, and everything is under version control. The measure of a finished engagement is that you don't need to call me.
